## Why we coat in-house

Coating is where fabricated steel waits. Hot-dip galvanising runs on the galvaniser's schedule, and a head frame fabricated in days can sit for weeks before it can go to site. We set up thermal coating at Bylands so steel from our workshop can be blasted, coated and loaded on our own programme.

### The process

1. **Blast.** Steel is grit blasted to remove mill scale, rust, weld spatter and contamination, and to give the coating a profile to key into.
2. **Check.** The blasted surface is checked for cleanliness and profile before any coating goes on.
3. **Coat.** A thermal spray metal coating is applied: zinc, zinc alloy or an alloy system, chosen for the exposure the steel will see.
4. **Seal.** A sealer or top coat is applied where the specification calls for one.

### Choosing the system

The right system depends on where the steel is going. Inland, coastal, marine and industrial sites sit in different atmospheric corrosivity categories, and each needs a different coating and thickness to reach the design life. Tell us the site location and the required life, and we quote the system to match rather than a blanket figure.

### Hot-dip galvanising

Some specifications call for hot-dip galvanising rather than thermal spray. We arrange it through galvanising partners, book the slot when the fabrication schedule is set, and manage transport both ways. You deal with one supplier for the fabricated and coated item.

### What is coated here

Steel from our [fabrication workshop](/services/steel-fabrication/) is coated at Bylands as standard, then delivered to site or held for our own crews. Coated items include head frames, mounts, monopole jackets, foundation cages and equipment frames.
