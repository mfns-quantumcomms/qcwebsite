## Why a hire company runs a workshop

A truck-mounted EWP is two machines: the platform and the truck under it. Both need servicing, and both need to be inspection-current before a unit is released to site. We run our own service division so the units on the [fleet register](/fleet/) are maintained by people who know each one, not by whichever dealer has a slot.

### What the workshop does

- **Scheduled servicing.** Platform and chassis services to the manufacturer's schedule, for Bronto and Ruthmann platforms and the trucks under them.
- **Inspections.** Periodic inspections and ten-year major inspections, with structural, hydraulic and electrical checks recorded against the unit.
- **Repairs.** Fault finding and repair of hydraulic, electrical and control systems, boom and cage repairs, and parts sourcing.
- **Records.** Every hire unit carries a current logbook and daily pre-start checks. Dry-hire units get a condition report at the start and end of the hire.

### Who does the work

Our diesel mechanics are on staff and based at the Bylands workshop and fleet yard, next to the fabrication and coating shop. Minor steel repairs can be fabricated, coated and refitted in the same yard.

<p class="notice">To be confirmed by the client before launch: whether servicing, inspections and major inspections are offered to third-party EWP owners; the inspection standard the workshop works to; and whether the service lead should be named on this page.</p>

### For hire clients

On a dry hire, your operator completes an induction on the unit before it is released. The unit leaves with its logbook and pre-hire condition report, and is inspected again at off-hire. See [how hire works](/fleet/how-hire-works/) for the process and the [hire terms](/hire-terms/) for conditions.
